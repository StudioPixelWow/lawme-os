B and C tie numerically; B owns distinctiveness and legal-fit (the Bible's spine),
C owns calm and scalability. The correct answer is not to pick one but to
**synthesize** — which is exactly what the Bible already describes.

## The recommended composition — "The Matter Room"

**B's milestone spine as the structure + A's Decision Core as the headline + C's
one-focus discipline and Context Dock.**

- The **Milestone Spine** is the backbone of the canvas (Option B), with the gold
  meridian on the current stage — the Bible's signature and the answer to "where has
  this matter been / where is it / what's next / what blocks the transition."
- The **Decision Core** is the expanded current node and the room's headline
  (Option A's clarity), concentrating all eight five-second answers + the one action.
- The **Context Dock** holds the Score Lens, the Intelligence Lenses, the nearest
  deadline, and team presence; selecting a stage, lens, or deadline re-aims the
  canvas (Option C's focus discipline, the Bible's Context Halo).
- **דינו** appears as seals on the nodes/objects he has findings for, plus an
  on-request assistant from the TopBar — never a permanent panel.

This passes the blur test (spine + meridian + state chip = unmistakably the LawME
Matter App), the one-hero law (the active node/Decision Core is the single hero),
and the two-accent law (gold meridian + one semantic state color).

## Why the others "lost"

- **Option A alone** demotes the spine to a strip, contradicting the Bible and
  making the screen feel like a command *dashboard* rather than a matter *room*. Its
  gift — the concentrated Decision Core headline — is **borrowed** into the active
  node.
- **Option C alone** demotes the spine to context; a legal matter's procedure is too
  central to be mere context. Its gifts — one-focus calm, the Context Dock, and
  re-aiming — are **borrowed** as the interaction model.

## What to borrow from each

- From A: the Decision Core as a concentrated, verb-ending headline with one CTA.
- From C: Esc-returns-one-level focus, the Context Dock, and canvas re-aiming on
  selection.
- From B: the spine itself as the spatial truth of the matter.

## Remaining risks (to prototype against)

1. **Spine-as-wizard risk** — the milestone track must read as a living spine
   (breathing "now" node, dashed future, risk pulse), not a setup stepper. Test with
   a long procedure (regional-court civil, 7 stages) and a short one (hearing-before-
   dismissal, 3–4 stages).
2. **Hero ambiguity** — the active node must obviously be the hero; test the
   five-second read on the blocked state and the healthy state.
3. **Density vs calm** — the densest workspace in LawME must stay "operational, not
   cramped"; the Reduction Review's Reduced Premium variant is the target.
4. **Dock overload** — lenses must open one at a time; never all expanded.

## What must be tested in the first prototype

- Five-second test on healthy vs blocked vs degraded matters.
- The spine with a missing-item diamond and a risk pulse on the transition.
- One primary action always above the fold, owner + due visible.
- דינו seal appearing only where there is a finding (silent otherwise).
- RTL correctness of the spine direction and the meridian.

Design **the Reduced Premium variant of the Matter Room first** (see Reduction
Review).
