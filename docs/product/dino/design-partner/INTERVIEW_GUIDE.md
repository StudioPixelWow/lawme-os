# Dino V1 — Post-Session Interview Guide

**Part 6 of the Evaluation Kit.** A semi-structured guide (~15 min) administered after **every** session. Ask open questions, listen more than you talk, and capture verbatim quotes. The goal is to hear *why* a lawyer would or wouldn't trust, adopt, and pay — not to defend the product.

> **Moderator rules:** never explain away a failure in the moment; never coach; don't lead ("wasn't that helpful?"). Sit with silence. Ask "why" three times. Record (with consent). One insight per sticky note for later affinity-mapping.

---

## 0. Warm-up (2 min)
- In one sentence, what did Dino feel like — a colleague, a search engine, or something else?
- On a scale of 1–10, how much would you trust it *today* for your labor-law work? (Then: "what would move it +2?")

## 1. What they trusted (3 min)
- Which answer felt the most trustworthy? What specifically made it feel that way?
- Did the citations/badges ("מאומת", "מבוסס חקיקה מאומתת") change your confidence? How?
- When Dino said "I can't answer this / this needs a fact" — did that increase or decrease your trust? Why?

## 2. What they didn't trust (3 min)
- Which answer made you doubt it? What was the tell?
- Was there a moment you thought "a junior who did this would get a talking-to"? What happened?
- Did anything feel confident-but-hollow? Where?

## 3. Where they hesitated (2 min)
- Point to a moment you paused before acting on an answer. What were you weighing?
- Would you have shown any of these answers to a client or the court *as-is*? Which, and why/why not?
- What would you have independently checked before relying — and would you still check it the 10th time?

## 4. What they expected (2 min)
- What did you expect Dino to do that it didn't?
- On the out-of-scope answers (severance, dismissal, hours…) — was declining acceptable, or a dealbreaker? Which doctrines *must* be covered before you'd adopt?
- What's the first thing you'd want it to draft or export for you?

## 5. Competitors & alternatives (2 min)
- What do you use today for these questions (Nevo / Takdin / Kol-Zchut / a colleague / your own memory)?
- What does that tool do **better** than Dino? What does Dino do better?
- Would Dino replace a browser tab you keep open, or sit alongside it?

## 6. What surprised them (1 min)
- What surprised you — good or bad?
- Anything Dino did that you didn't know you wanted?

## 7. Adoption & willingness to pay (close, 2 min)
- If this covered your top 3 doctrines at today's quality, would you use it weekly? Daily?
- Would you pay for it? What feels fair — per lawyer/month, per matter, firm license? What number?
- Would you recommend it to a colleague in labor law? Can we mention you as a design partner?
- The one change that would most increase the chance you'd adopt?

---

## Probe bank (use as needed)
- "Say more about that." · "What did you expect instead?" · "What would have to be true for you to rely on it?" · "Walk me through what you'd do next with this answer." · "Where did you feel the risk?" · "If a colleague sent you this, what would you say?"

## Red-flag phrases to capture verbatim (early trust-death signals)
- "That's not the section." / "That number is old." / "It made that up." / "It's too confident." / "I'd never file this." / "It's just Google." → each maps to a **P0/P1** in the [severity model](./DESIGN_PARTNER_PROGRAM.md#part-7--severity-model); log the question ID.

## Green-flag phrases to capture (adoption signals)
- "It caught the missing fact." / "The citation is exactly right." / "I like that it refused." / "This would save me an hour." / "Can I use this on a real file?" / "When can I have it?"

---

## Synthesis (after each partner, 10 min)
Cluster quotes into: **trusted / distrusted / hesitated / expected / surprised / would-pay**. Tag each with a severity and a question ID. Feed P0/P1 items straight into the regression backlog and the corpus-expansion priority list (rank doctrines by how often partners hit "out of scope" and called it a dealbreaker). Roll into the [scorecard](./EVALUATION_SCORECARD.md) program summary.
